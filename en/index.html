The story

Born from a real DJ problem

I'm Hervé, the founder of TrackZapDJ. I started DJing in clubs in the late '80s, on vinyl and our beloved MK2s: New Wave first, then House, New Beat and Electro. I never really let go of the decks, even when my career took over: I stayed a passionate amateur.

During Covid, I finally switched to a controller — a DDJ-1000 with Rekordbox. Gigabytes of MP3s piled up from all kinds of sources, and my digital collection filled up with duplicates.

There are full software suites, packed with features, that also handle duplicates. But for my use, they were too expensive and didn't quite fit this particular need. So I built TrackZapDJ. It's an independent project: the free version is meant to be useful on its own, and purchases pay for hosting, maintenance and new features.

🛡️ Built with caution

✋

Nothing changes without you

Scanning changes nothing. You approve group by group, simulate, then type “SUPPRIMER” (delete) to apply.

♻️

Backup and roll back

Before every cleanup, your database is backed up and removed files are set aside. You can roll back any of the last 5 cleanups.

🚦

Safety checks

The cleanup is blocked while Rekordbox is open, and a file still used by a kept track is never moved.

🏠

100% local

Everything is processed on your computer. Your library is never sent anywhere.

To be honest: these safeguards greatly reduce the risks, but can't remove them entirely. Before a big cleanup, keep your own backup too.

FAQ

You might be wondering…

Will TrackZapDJ delete my remixes or extended versions?

No, not without your approval. The main scan compares artist and title: an “Extended Mix” or a “Radio Edit” with a different title is not grouped with the original. The musical scan (key, BPM, duration) can bring similar versions together: the confidence level tells you so, and nothing is deleted until you approve the group.

What's the difference between the free and the paid version?

The free version finds duplicates, suggests which track to keep, simulates the cleanup and exports the full analysis to an Excel file, without changing anything. The paid version will apply the cleanup: just one copy kept, data attached to the kept track, backup and roll back.

What happens to my cues, playlists, history and tags?

During cleanup, your playlists, history, Automix list and tags move to the kept track, so you can still replay a past set. If the kept track is already in a playlist, the duplicate entry is simply removed. Your hot cues and memory cues are copied to the kept track when both files have the same duration (within one second), without duplicating the ones already there.

Are duplicates also removed from my hard drive?

Yes, with the paid version, but not straight away. Removed files leave your music folder and go to the backup folder of your choice, so you can roll back. They are permanently deleted once that cleanup drops out of the history of the last 5; TrackZapDJ warns you first.

Does it find duplicate files that aren't in Rekordbox?

No. TrackZapDJ works on the tracks in your Rekordbox collection and the files they point to. A file sitting on your drive that was never imported into Rekordbox is not scanned.

Does the free version change my library?

No. It reads your library to analyse it and shows you what could be done. No database and no file is modified.

Could I lose data?

TrackZapDJ is designed to limit that risk: simulation, manual approval, automatic backup, files set aside rather than erased, and roll back. Still, no tool can guarantee zero risk, so keep your own backup before a big cleanup.

Is my library sent over the Internet?

No. The whole analysis runs on your computer. Your tracks and your database stay with you.

Does TrackZapDJ run on Mac?

Not yet. The first version is available on PC only. The app is designed to run on both PC and Mac: the Mac version will follow.

Is the app available in English?

Not yet: it is in French for now, but it will be available in English at the official launch.
